= Fluid Buffers and water towers
:description: How a Fluid Buffer sets pressure, fills and drains, and how to use one as a water tower.

== A tank's surface sets the pressure

A Fluid Buffer pushes with the height of its liquid surface.
The pressure downstream of it is set by that surface, not by what is pushing into it.
To get fluid past a tank to somewhere higher, put a pump after the tank.

* *A full Fluid Buffer takes no more,* and whatever feeds it backs off.
* *A tank feeding a downhill pipe holds some liquid back.*
Liquid entering a part-full pipe passes only what the depth over the pipe's mouth allows.
So the tank stands at the level its outlet needs for the flow it gives, about 1.6 m to pass 250 m³/min.
A tank that never seems to drain below that level is working.

== Water towers

A Fluid Buffer above the machines holds the pressure up through start-up and through any surge later.
How to place one, charge it, and open it is in xref:starting-up.adoc#_water_towers[Starting up a network].

== Storage before pumps

On a balanced network, a Fluid Buffer between the producers and the pumps lets a surge draw on the buffer instead of the pumps' suction.
See xref:starting-up.adoc#_a_balanced_network[A balanced network].

== Gas in a Fluid Buffer

A Fluid Buffer on a gas line stores gas under pressure, and fills slowly.
When bringing a gas line up, valve the buffer off and open it last.

A new or flushed Fluid Buffer on a gas line starts at atmospheric pressure.
