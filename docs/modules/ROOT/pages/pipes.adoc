= Pipes
:description: Pipe capacity, friction, fill, and what the pipe panel shows.

== No flow rating

Vanilla caps a Mk1 pipe at 300 m³/min and a Mk2 at 600.
FicstFluids has no cap.
What limits a pipe is friction: flow costs pressure along its length, so a short run can carry far more than a long one.
The only hard cap in a network is a valve's flow limit.

| Pipe | Bore | Holds per meter

| Mk1

| Mk2
| 1.5 m
| about 1.8 m³
|===

A Mk2 pipe loses far less to friction than a Mk1 at the same flow.
Two pipes side by side split the flow, and each loses about a quarter as much.

== Pressure needs a full pipe

A liquid pipe gives no pressure until it is full.
While pipes and machine buffers are filling, the liquid goes into them, not into pressure.

== The fill shown on the panel

A pipe's fill is an estimate of how the pipe would look, worked out from its shape and flow.
It is not the amount of liquid the network counts.
So the percentages across a line won't add up to the network's total, and a dip can hold about twice what its pipes' readings suggest.

A pipe running downhill into something with room (a nearly empty tank or platform) doesn't fill.
The liquid runs down it as a stream, and the pipe shows part full while carrying its full flow.

== Flushing

Flushing a pipe empties its whole run at once, and the network carries on.
On a gas line, flushing vents the run to atmospheric pressure.

Flushing a whole gas network empties it to atmospheric and pauses it, compressors stopped, until a fluid arrives again.
Then it rebuilds itself and carries on.

== Placing a pump on a full pipe

Placing a pump on a full pipe throws away that pipe's contents: about 13.9 m³ for 11 m of Mk1 pipe.
