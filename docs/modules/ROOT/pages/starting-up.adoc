= Starting up a network
:description: Why a network can fail to come up to pressure, and how to start one reliably.

== The rule: pressurize first, then bring machines on slowly

The reliable way to start any FicstFluids network is to let it fill and come up to pressure with little or nothing drawing.
Then switch the consumers on a few at a time.

The rest of this page is that rule in its different forms.

== Why start-up is hard

At start-up every pipe and every machine buffer is empty.
Every machine is refilling at up to twice its rate.
So the network is asked for far more than it will need once it's running, and the pressure may never build.

Joining two networks does the same: the new machines' empty buffers can take all the flow.

Friction makes it worse: the farther machines get less pressure.

TIP: The machines' buffers show how it is going.
A buffer still filling means the network is still filling it.

== Liquid: the standby sequence

. Put some or all of the machines on standby.
. Let the pressure build.
. Switch them back on a few at a time.

Once the network is full and the buffers are topped up, it supplies them all.

=== An exactly matched chain

One machine feeding another that uses exactly what it makes (one Refinery into another) settles at a low pressure, about 5 m at the consumer's inlet rather than the producer's 10 m.

While the consumer's buffer isn't full, it asks for twice its rate.
The producer can't give more than its own rate, so the line sits where the consumer's part-rate draw equals it.
The consumer still gets all it uses and runs at full speed, so the report doesn't flag it.

To bring the pressure up, put the consumer on standby for a moment.
Its buffer fills, the line comes up to the producer's pressure, and it stays there when switched back on.

== Water towers

A Fluid Buffer above the machines holds the pressure up from its own water while the network fills, and through any surge later.
Its water surface must stand above the machines' inlets by at least their desired inlet pressure (8 m for a Refinery), plus the friction on the way.

=== Charge the tower before opening it

A tower whose outlet is open from the start fills no faster than the network leaves it water.
At start-up the empty pipes and refilling machines take everything, so the tower may never fill.

. Close a valve on the tower's outlet (set its limit to 0), or leave the outlet unconnected.
. Let the tower fill. The whole supply goes into it.
. *Open it part way.* Set the valve near the supply's rate, so the network fills at about what the supply puts back and the tower stays near full.
. Raise the limit step by step, or open it fully once the network is up.

CAUTION: The tower must hold enough to fill the pipes downstream and cover the machines' refill, or it empties and the start-up shortage is back.
A meter of pipe holds about 1.2 m³ on Mk1 and 1.8 m³ on Mk2.

=== A tower at the factory for a distant source

When the source is far away (wells or extractors at the end of a long line), put the tower at the factory end.
To start up, shut the tower's outlet and let the long line fill it; then open it to the machines, part way at first.
Everything you need to control is at the factory, and you don't have to travel out along the line.

== Long supply lines: know when it's full

. Put all but one or two machines on standby.
. Open the long supply pipe's panel.
. While the line is filling, more goes in at its inlet than comes out at its outlet.
. Once the inlet and outlet flows are equal, the line is full and will pressurize. Start the other machines.

== Pumps on a thick liquid

A pump whose suction is negative and close to its limit slows itself to stay clear of cavitation.
On a thick liquid such as Heavy Oil Residue, each change in its speed changes the friction on the suction line, and the line won't settle.

Once the system has pressurized and the pump's suction is no longer negative, it runs far more steadily.
Feed such pumps from a tank above them, or put them right at the source.

== A balanced network

A network whose producers make about what its machines use runs without trouble once it is up.
Getting it there takes care, because it can settle two ways:

* *Buffers full:* the machines take only what they use, and the pumps run at full lift.
* *Buffers refilling:* the machines ask for up to twice their rate, demand outruns supply, the pressure at the pumps' suction falls, and the pumps slow to protect themselves.
The network then recovers only as fast as any surplus refills the buffers, which can take far longer than anyone will wait.

Anything that makes many machines refill at once can tip it into the second: a start-up, machines switched back on after a pause, a connection to another network.

.Example: twelve Blenders feeding 42 Oil Refineries
====
Twelve Blenders (1,280 m³/min) fed 42 Refineries through two Mk2 pumps, with no storage and about 4% to spare.
At full pressure the Refineries sat near 70 to 76 m.
After a surge the main pump ran at 44% speed with its suction at −5.6 m, its Refineries at 5 to 6 m, and it was still there 8½ minutes later.
Pausing the Refineries until the pressure built brought it back, and it then held.
====

*Fixes:* start it up with the standby sequence above, which also gets it running again after a surge.
Or give it storage, between the producers and the pumps or as a water tower, so a surge draws on a Fluid Buffer instead of the pumps' suction.

== Start-up shortage or an over-capacity network?

Standby fixes a start-up shortage, not a network that can't supply its machines.

At start-up the shortage is temporary, because refilling buffers ask for up to twice their rate.
But if the machines' combined use is more than the network can deliver at their inlet pressure, switching them back on returns it to the same low pressure.
That needs more supply, a pump, less friction (shorter, Mk2 or a second pipe), or fewer machines.
A water tower is the same: it bridges a temporary shortage and drains in a permanent one.

*To tell them apart,* give it a minute after switching the machines back on:

* Buffers staying full and pressure staying up: it was start-up.
* Pressure sinking back and buffers draining: the network is over capacity.
