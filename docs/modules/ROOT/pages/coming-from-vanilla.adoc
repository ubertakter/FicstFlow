= Coming from vanilla
:description: What changes when FicstFluids takes over an existing factory, and what to do about it.

If you install FicstFluids in an existing save, some of your networks will stop or run slow.
That is expected: they were built for vanilla's rules.
This page lists what changed and where to read more.

== What changes

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
| | Vanilla | FicstFluids

| Pipe capacity
| Mk1 300 m³/min, Mk2 600 m³/min
| No cap. Friction limits the flow, so length matters. (xref:pipes.adoc[])

| Pump
| A fixed head lift (22 m for a Mk1)
| A pump curve: up to 32.7 m (Mk1) or 65.3 m (Mk2) at no flow, less as the flow rises. (xref:pumps.adoc[])

| Machine inlets
| Take whatever reaches them
| Need pressure: below a minimum they draw nothing. (xref:machines.adoc[])

| Machine outlets
| Push into anything
| Push at a fixed pressure, 10 m of head for liquids. Returning water to a pressurized network needs a pump.

| Manifolds
| Share fluid badly, so machines are often piped one into the next
| A pressurized manifold delivers to every machine on it.

| Valves
| Used to tame sloshing and share flow
| One-way, with a flow limit that is a hard cap. Leftover throttling may now starve machines. (xref:valves.adoc[])

| Gas
| Flows like a liquid
| Compressible. Gas machines need MPa, so gas lines need compressors. (xref:gas-networks.adoc[])

| Freight platforms
| A loading platform still pushes out of a connected outlet
| Only one side works at a time. (xref:freight.adoc[])
|===

== What to check in an existing factory

. *Gas lines will stop.*
Vanilla's gas lines have no compressors, and what gas is left in them is far too little to give gas machines their pressure.
Add a compressor (a pipeline pump on the gas line), then give the line time to fill.
A long line can take half an hour.
. *Long or high liquid lines may need pumps.*
Add up the height to climb and the friction, and compare it with what the source pushes.
See xref:liquid-networks.adoc[].
. *Look at throttled valves.*
A valve set low to work around vanilla still caps the flow at that setting.
Open it up, or take it out.
. *Water returned by machines* (Silica, Aluminum chains) may back up.
It needs a pump to get back into the supply.
. *Restart big networks carefully.*
A network can fail to come up to pressure at start-up even if it would run once it was up.
See xref:starting-up.adoc[].

The xref:report.adoc[Network Status report] (L by default) lists every network with a problem, so start there.

== What you no longer need

* *Piping one machine straight into the next.*
Vanilla builds often feed one Refinery directly into another, because vanilla's manifolds share badly.
In FicstFluids the producers can feed one manifold and the consumers draw from it.
Watch the head budget: see xref:liquid-networks.adoc#_manifolds[Manifolds].
* *Valves to balance branches.*
They do no harm left wide open.
* *Pipes in parallel just for capacity.*
A pipe has no rating; a second pipe only helps by cutting the friction.
