= Freight platforms and truck stations
:description: How fluid freight platforms and truck stations fill, empty and carry gas.

A Fluid Freight Platform and a Fluid Truck Station basically work the same way.

== It fills like a tank

A loading platform needs no inlet pressure.
It fills whenever the supply's head stands above its surface.
Its inlets enter at the bottom, the upper one included, so topping up a nearly full platform needs about 6.5 m of head above its lower ports.
Its inlets can't drain it.

== It empties like an extractor

An unloading platform's outlets supply liquid at a steady 10 m of head until it is empty.
Then they stop, and start again once it holds a little more.

An unloading station's 10 m may not fill a Fluid Buffer.
The buffer stops at the level that balances it, and the station keeps the rest; one level with the station's ports stopped at about 81%.
To fill it, add a pump or lower the buffer.

== Running machines from an unloading station

An unloading station keeps supplying its line while a truck docks.
The delivery lands without interrupting the flow.

When the station runs empty, its outlets stop and the line stops flowing.
Each machine runs on its own internal buffer until that is used up, and then stops.
On a level pipe, the pipe still reads 100% full: the liquid stands in it, but nothing is pushing it.
A full pipe isn't a supply.
If the pipe drops toward the machine, the liquid in it keeps the machine going a little longer, until what is left no longer gives the machine its minimum inlet head (4 m, see xref:machines.adoc[]).

When the next truck delivers, the station supplies again, and the line and its machines restart by themselves.

To keep machines running between trucks, deliver more often, or put a Fluid Buffer between the station and the machines, low enough for the station's 10 m to fill it.

== Only one side works at a time

In load mode a platform takes from its inlets and its outlets are shut; in unload mode, the reverse.

NOTE: This differs from vanilla, where a loading platform still pushes fluid out of any connected outlet.

* *Train platforms:* while a train is docking, both sides are shut.
* *Truck stations:* nothing shuts during the 9 s dock.
The whole transfer lands at the end, just before the truck leaves, so the truck reads empty until then.

== A transfer fills only the free space

Loading tops the wagon up to full and leaves the rest on the platform.
Unloading tops the platform up and leaves the rest in the wagon, so the train leaves part-loaded.
Filling a platform can take more than one train.
This is vanilla's rule.

== Gas

Gas wagons and platforms carry gas under pressure, up to the compressors' unloading pressure, about 91 MPa.
Their panels show the mass of gas held and its pressure, not a volume.

* *Filling* a platform that far takes a compressor on its inlet.
* *A train takes everything from a gas platform in one go.*
The pressures don't balance: the wagon takes all it has room for, and the platform can end at vacuum.
* *An empty gas platform is at vacuum,* about −101 kPa gauge, new or just flushed.
The first gas rushes in until the pressures meet.
* *A gas platform's outlet runs down as it empties.*
It supplies at the platform's own pressure, so the flow slows and stops once that meets the line's.
To empty it further takes a compressor on the outlet.
* *A gas line delivers less than it takes in while its pressure rises.*
The difference is gas packing into the pipes, not a loss.

== Changing a platform's fluid

A platform that holds one fluid won't take another.
Connecting a new line leaves its ports shut, even with the old line gone.

*Flush it.*
That discards everything it holds, however full, and it is then ready for any fluid, gas or liquid.
A flushed gas platform's inlet can stay shut for a few seconds before it reopens and fills.
