= Gas networks
:description: Compressors, filling a gas line, boosters, and bringing a big line up in sections.

Read the xref:primer.adoc#_gas_is_different[primer's gas section] first.
The short version: gas compresses, so a gas line stores gas as pressure and takes time to fill.

NOTE: Gas properties are still being tuned.
The way gas networks behave won't change, but the times on this page are estimates and will probably get shorter.

== Gas lines need compressors

Gas machines work at MPa.
A Blender's nitrogen inlet draws nothing below about 5.5 MPa gauge (800 psig) and wants 6.9 MPa (1,000 psig).
That is far above what any gas source supplies; a Resource Well's nitrogen comes out at about 1.45 MPa.

A pipeline pump placed on a gas line works as a *compressor*.
It multiplies the absolute pressure at its suction by up to its ratio limit.
See xref:compressors.adoc[] for the numbers.

TIP: Because a compressor multiplies absolute pressure, it can do much more with a high suction pressure than a low one.

== Filling takes time

Pressure appears only once the line holds enough gas, and it fills no faster than the producers supply, less what is drawn off.

A compressor at the far end of a line that isn't full pulls its suction below atmospheric and runs near its ratio limit, moving little, until the line fills.

.Example: four wells down a 1,075 m nitrogen line
====
Four Resource Well Extractors (120 std m³/min each) fed a 1,075 m line to a Mk2 compressor and a Blender.
The Blender draws nothing below 5.5 MPa, which one Mk2 reaches only from about 600 kPa at its suction.
The first gas reached the Blender after about half an hour.
====

*The quicker layout:* put the compressor at the consumer's end.
Then the line need only reach the compressor's suction pressure, not the consumer's.

== One line for all the wells

All the wells at a nitrogen extraction point can share one output line.
A single Mk2 pipe carries far more than a pressurizer's wells make.

.Example
====
Two Resource Well Extractors, 600 std m³/min together, ran down one line to a Mk2 compressor taking 585 std m³/min at about 1 MPa suction, and delivering 8.3 MPa to the Blenders.
====

== Long lines: boosters

Gas loses pressure to friction as it flows, and the loss feeds on itself.
As the pressure falls the gas expands, moves faster, and loses pressure faster still.
So a line that carries a lot of gas a long way arrives at a low pressure.

A compressor partway along, a *booster*, lifts the pressure again; the gas past it is denser and slower and loses less.
Real gas pipelines work this way, with compressor stations along their length.

* *The wells set the start.*
Their outlet holds at most about 1.45 MPa, so a line straight from them can lose no more than that before its far end reaches atmospheric.
A compressor near the wells raises the whole line's pressure, so the same flow loses less.
* *Put the booster where the suction is still well above atmospheric,* not at the far end of a line that has run down.
* *Compressors in series multiply:* two Mk2s reach about 7.9 × 7.9 times the first one's absolute suction pressure, less the line's losses between them.

== Bring a big line up in sections

The time to reach a machine's pressure grows with the volume being filled.
So split the line with valves and let the part nearest the compressor come up first; its machines start running while the rest waits.

. Valve off anything that adds volume without needing gas yet: a Fluid Buffer, or a machine whose inlet needs far more pressure than the others.
. Close a valve just past the first few consumers.
. Open each next valve *only part way*.
A gas valve holds the flow limit you set, in std m³/min.
Set it to what the producers make, minus what the running machines take.
Opened fully, the next section's low pressure pulls the running section below its machines' minimum and they stop.
. Open the Fluid Buffer last.

.Example: a Rocket Fuel line
====
Two Blenders fed a Mk2 compressor, fifteen Fuel-Powered Generators and a 400 m³ Fluid Buffer: about 1,450 m³ in all.
Filling all of it to the generators' 5.5 MPa takes hours; each 100 m³ section takes about a quarter of an hour.
====

== Very high pressures: the plasma chain

A Particle Accelerator on Dark Matter Residue draws nothing below 48.3 MPa gauge (7,000 psig) and reaches full rate at 68.9 MPa.
That takes *at least two stages* of compression: from a Converter's 2.8 MPa, one Mk2 reaches only about 18 MPa, and two in series about 107 MPa.

Compressing these hot plasmas costs far more power than nitrogen.
The two Mk2 stages an Accelerator needs draw about 12.7 MW together.

NOTE: The plasma chain's pressures are under review and may change.

== Gas in an existing save

Vanilla gas lines stop when FicstFluids is installed.
Vanilla has no pressure, so its gas machines ran on whatever its pipes held.
FicstFluids keeps that gas, but in a long line it is far too little, and those lines have no compressors.

Add compressors, then give the line time to fill.
